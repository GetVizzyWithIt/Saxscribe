What each score measures

A transcription can go wrong in several separate ways, so no single number says how good it is. Each score below checks one of them against an answer key. Pick it with the buttons under Setups compared.

DimensionScoreWhat it countsKnown weak spots
Hearing the notesSax foundShare of the key’s notes we wrote, with the right start (50 ms) and pitch. Quiet sax under the band; fast runs; altissimo.
Not adding notesSax purityShare of our notes that the key has. Notes in rests are mostly bleed from piano or guitar. Piano solos on band records; ghost notes in rests.
Both togetherNote F1Found and purity combined, and the note’s end must be within 20% too. Long held notes split in two; note ends.
Fast and short notesShort notes foundShare of the key’s notes under 0.12 s that we wrote. Guards against a score that rises by dropping hard notes. Grace notes and ghost notes.
Timing and pitchOnset recall, PitchNote starts found (any pitch); pitch right on the notes found. Scoops and bends read as another pitch.
Bar placementBars rightShare of the key’s bar lines we put in the same place. On the written score only; the notes are not affected. Tunes the beat tracker follows at half speed (fast Weimar records).
Rhythm writingNotes in the right spotOf the notes we found (same pitch, start within 150 ms), the share written at the key’s beat position, allowing a whole-bar offset. Light swing; ballads and rubato; onsets more than 50 ms off.
